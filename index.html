About

CosmosMap

CosmosMap is an interactive WebGPU map and simulation for exploring the solar system, nearby stars, the Milky Way, galaxies, dust, constellations, and visual black-hole lensing.

It was created by Bekir Dag for his children, so they can learn about the universe by moving through it instead of only reading about it.

The project was built with help from Claude and Codex 5.5. It is open for contributions: bug reports, data corrections, visualization improvements, and educational ideas are welcome.

Limits & Assumptions

What CosmosMap Shows

CosmosMap prioritizes an explorable educational view over a fully exhaustive astrophysics engine. Many layers are visual references only and are deliberately kept out of the N-body force calculation.

Data Sources

  • Solar-system starting vectors come from cached NASA/JPL Horizons data.
  • Nearby visible stars use a 100,000-star HYG 4.2 render buffer.
  • Exoplanet host stars are searchable from a NASA Exoplanet Archive snapshot.
  • Constellation lines and names use cached NASA SVS Deep Star Maps source data.
  • Galactic dust uses procedural cloud instances seeded from the NASA/GSFC LAMBDA Meisner-Finkbeiner 2015 E(B-V) all-sky dust map; the public reddening directions are projected through a Milky Way disk model, not rendered as a Sun-centered shell or treated as true 3D extinction distances.
  • Galaxy rendering combines real named galaxy entries with procedural deep-field fill.

Physics Assumptions

  • The 33 solar-system bodies are integrated as the active simulation state.
  • Catalog stars, Milky Way stars, galaxies, dust, constellations, and nebulas are render-only layers.
  • The Sun is not fixed at the origin in the physics state; Horizons vectors include its motion.
  • A simplified local galactic-frame acceleration is visual/educational, not a full Milky Way potential model.
  • Sagittarius A* lensing is a visual approximation, not a relativistic ray-tracing solver.

Rendering And Culling

  • Frustum culling remains in the GPU shaders so off-screen billboards are skipped safely.
  • Occlusion culling is intentionally avoided because empty space makes object-over-object hiding misleading.
  • Screen-density reduction hides crowded body clusters when many neighboring objects compress into a few pixels.
  • Large catalogs use octant-spread LOD caps so star and galaxy reductions stay distributed across the sky.
  • Actual brightness enables HDR star rendering, spectral color, and bloom for a realistic visual impression; it is not photometric proof.
